# Question contracts and references

A question record names the retrieval need before inspecting provider performance.
`provider_query` is the exact common search sentence. Its `contract` preserves all
grading requirements: qualifying predicates, source material, independence, language,
temporal requirements, any as-of interpretation, permitted background context and
requested facets. Facets name requested information across retrieval; a result does
not need every facet to qualify. An if-present requirement is not a universal quota.

Records also carry a stable `question_id`, `revision`, `family_id`, `need_id` and
explicit geography. Different wordings of one need share its need/family identity.
Do not remove family members from a comparison and call the remaining weighting
complete. Do not generate or rewrite real bank questions in response to provider
performance. Bundled fictional questions are software fixtures only.

Keep four date meanings separate: publication, the requested event/stage, historical
state, and when a fact was publicly available. Collection/capture time is provenance.
Updates, crawling, indexing and unclear native ages cannot become publication or
event proof merely because a field contains a date. Preserve the original value,
precision, timezone and mapping. The calendar comparison policy handles whole-day
windows without inventing an exact midnight or timezone. A coarse interval crossing
a consequential boundary remains unknown.

Temporal requirements explicitly declare `basis`, `id`, `start_inclusive` and
`end_exclusive`. Event requirements also declare the required `kind` and `stage`.
An as-of requirement declares historical state, knowledge or both, its target/cutoff,
timezone, original question phrase and cutoff boundary as applicable. Resolve that
meaning before judging. Source eligibility, independence, geography, stage and
historical availability remain separate conditions.

Answer facts require own returned title/snippet support. Declared native metadata
supports eligibility, while descriptive URLs may support concrete leads or publisher
identity; a URL cannot confirm an answer fact or date. Source decisions need item
evidence as well as publisher identity. Geography must concern the requested entity
and role; related markets, incorporation or a branch address do not establish HQ.

Shared references derive only from reviewed supported answer findings. Leads remain
separate and do not create answer facts. No historical answer, capture or label is
imported into a new collection. Stable report/event identities need reviewed input;
the runner does not invent identity equivalence from a grade or similar wording.
Breadth metrics stay pending when those identities are unavailable. Question facets
can be reported only when supported by the selected own-evidence judgment.

For a public question projection, preserve every grading-relevant contract field,
geography and original query wording. Keep private provenance and historical answer
facts separate. Compare the projection with the private source contracts and review
the actual bytes before publishing. A search sentence alone is not a runnable
benchmark contract.
